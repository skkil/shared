## 1. Business plans and PSST

Korean government startup programs (예비창업패키지, 초기창업패키지, and many university and ministry programs) use the **PSST** structure:

- **Problem (문제인식):** the need for the item; domestic and global market status and problems; why this item is needed to solve them.
- **Solution (실현가능성):** the plan to develop or refine the idea into a product or service; differentiation and competitiveness; the plan for spending the grant.
- **Scale-up (성장전략):** competitor analysis and market entry strategy; business model (how it makes money); investment and funding strategy for expansion; overall roadmap, including mid- to long-term social value plans.
- **Team (팀 구성):** the founder's capabilities (development, execution, results); team members' capabilities and partners.

Rules that cost points or disqualify when ignored:
- Use the program's official template for the current year; don't delete or reorder required sections. Images and tables may be added.
- Respect page limits (the 2026 예비창업패키지 limited the plan to about 10 pages); delete the template's guide text before submitting.
- Map every evaluation criterion in the announcement (공고문) to a section before writing, and address each explicitly.
- Problem sections are scored on objective evidence of the pain and market, not a list of inconveniences: use sourced statistics (KOSIS, industry reports) and primary evidence (interviews, pilots, letters of intent).
- Budget plans follow the program's allowed expense categories (사업비 집행 비목).
- Check eligibility rules (business registration status, prior closures, concurrent program participation) before investing writing time.

## 2. Pitch narrative and investor memos

Structure the story as a change in the world: the shift that's happening, the winners and losers it creates, the promised land for customers, how the product gets them there, evidence it's working (traction, pilots, quotes), why this team, and the ask. Numbers are sourced and labeled; risks are named with mitigations. Rebecca designs the deck from Jennifer's storyline (one message per slide, readable from titles alone). An investor memo is the same argument in prose, with a market section, competitive landscape, business model, traction, team, use of funds, and key risks.

## 3. Financial projections

Build as a spreadsheet (read the xlsx skill first) with: an assumptions sheet (every input labeled with evidence and a range), a revenue model by driver (users × conversion × price, not a growth percentage), costs (people, infrastructure, AI inference per user, tools, marketing), scenarios (low, base, high), break-even, and runway. Keep formulas live so the human can change an assumption and see the result. Not financial or tax advice; say so where it matters.

## 4. Grants and programs

Find relevant programs (K-Startup announcements, ministry and agency programs, university 창업지원단, foundations, open-source and research grants internationally). For each candidate: eligibility, amount, obligations (matching funds, reporting), deadline, evaluation criteria, and fit. Keep a tracker with due dates and status; propose which to pursue, with reasons. Draft application sections mapped to the criteria. Submission and anything signed are the human's.

## 5. Proposals

Partnership proposals, B2B proposals (제안서), RFP responses, and industry-academia collaboration (산학협력) proposals: the partner's problem in their words, the proposed solution and scope, deliverables and timeline, team, price and terms, why us, and next steps. For RFPs, answer every requirement in the RFP's own order and numbering, with a compliance matrix. Anything contractual is flagged for legal review.

## 6. Growth model

Describe how users arrive and return as **loops**, not just a funnel: a user does something that brings in another user or brings themselves back (content loops: user output gets indexed and found; referral loops; collaboration loops: users invite teammates to work; paid loops: revenue funds acquisition). For each loop: the steps, the conversion at each step `[measured]` or `[estimate]`, the cycle time, and the experiments to strengthen the weakest step. Pick the one or two loops that fit the product; most products have one that matters.

## 7. Search and store visibility

- **Google SEO:** keyword research tied to the jobs users search for, pages that answer those queries well, technical health (speed, indexability), and links earned from useful content.
- **Naver:** Naver's results favor its own properties (blogs, cafes, 지식iN) alongside web documents; register the site with Naver Search Advisor (서치어드바이저), and plan content for Naver blogs and cafes where Korean users research. Naver DataLab and the search ad keyword tool give Korean keyword demand.
- **App store optimization (App Store, Google Play):** title and subtitle keywords, screenshots that show the core value in the first two frames, ratings and review responses, localized listings (Korean listing written natively, not translated), and app vitals (poor crash and ANR rates reduce Google Play visibility; see `observability.md`).

## 8. Launch campaigns

A channel plan with timing, owners, and the message per channel. Global: Product Hunt, Hacker News (Show HN), relevant subreddits, X, LinkedIn, newsletters, press. Korean: Disquiet, GeekNews (news.hada.io), OKKY and Velog for developer products, relevant Naver cafes, KakaoTalk open chats, Everytime for student products, Korean tech press. Each community has norms: read the rules, contribute before promoting, and never use fake accounts or coordinated upvotes. A messaging brief goes to Rebecca (visuals) and Vanessa (docs and launch notes). Posting is gated.

## 9. Content and community

A content calendar tied to the jobs and keywords users care about and to product milestones; formats chosen per channel (tutorials, case studies, changelogs, short video). Community strategy where it fits the product: a Discord or KakaoTalk open chat for power users, office hours, a public roadmap or feedback board, recognition for contributors. Measure community by activity that helps users (questions answered, contributions), not member counts.

## 10. Lifecycle and retention

Onboarding sequences that lead to the activation event; lifecycle messages triggered by behavior (stalled setup, first success, inactivity), each tied to a metric; win-back plays for churned users with a reason to return. In Korea, marketing messages fall under the Information and Communications Network Act rules (prior consent for advertising messages, an "(광고)" label, opt-out, and separate consent for night-time sending), and KakaoTalk business messages require a business channel and approved templates; have these checked before launch. Every message has an opt-out and a frequency cap.
